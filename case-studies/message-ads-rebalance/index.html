Paid media audit · Meta message ads

More conversations on a smaller budget

One creative was taking three quarters of an ad set’s budget at the worst cost per lead in the account. I paused it, rebuilt the creative bench and moved the money to what was already converting. Fifteen days later the account was producing more conversations on a quarter less spend.

Role Paid media, sole operator Client Security doors & gates, Argentina (anonymized) Channel Meta → Instagram Direct Window 9 Sep – 8 Oct 2026

The client

Security doors and gates. Three years on the account.

High-ticket installations, sold one conversation at a time. Every lead arrives as an Instagram Direct message, so the account optimises for conversations started — not for purchases. The client never reports back which conversations became sales, which is why cost per conversation is the KPI I can actually defend.

I administer three ad accounts for this client and run them end to end on my own — strategy, creative, optimisation and reporting. No team, no account manager in between. This is one of those accounts, and it had been running the same way since March. Nobody asked for a review, which is usually when an account is worth reviewing.

The audit

One ad had most of the money and the worst cost in the account.

  • Three quarters of the ad set’s budget sat on one creative — ARS 523,736 of 721,064 — delivering conversations at 2.5× the account average.
  • Two creatives were spending and returning nothing, and a third had a single conversation to show for its spend.
  • The creative history was unreadable. New videos had been swapped inside existing ads, so one ad name covered several different pieces and no before/after could be reconstructed from the account itself.
  • Budget followed the original setup, not performance. The expensive audience held the larger daily budget because that is how it was built in March.

The real finding

The account was not losing money because of the creatives it had. It was losing money because the worst creative had the most budget, and nothing in the setup was going to correct that on its own.

The change

I moved the money to what was already converting.

  1. 23 Sep
    Paused the budget holder

    Its spend was released to the creatives already converting below the ad set average.

  2. 24 Sep
    Rebuilt the creative bench

    Nine creatives into test — four door-focused reels, a multi-product cut, four engagement variants — each launched as a new ad instead of a swap inside an old one.

  3. 25 Sep
    Shifted weight to the cheap audience

    The interest-based gate campaign kept its budget while the broad audience lost 40% of its spend. Conversations decided the split, not impressions.

  4. 1 Oct
    Cut the losers, kept three

    Every variant above the ad set average was paused once it had delivered enough conversations to read. Three stayed live.

  5. 8 Oct
    Read the result at equal windows

    Fifteen days before the change against fifteen days after — same account, same objective, same attribution setting.

Those 284 conversations would have cost ARS 1,417,773 at the old efficiency. They cost 881,663 — ARS 536,110, about USD 345, of spend that did not have to be made. Link click-through rate rose in both campaigns over the same window.

The check

Both campaigns got cheaper on their own.

The obvious objection to an account-level improvement is that it is an averaging trick: move budget to the cheap campaign and the blended number falls without anything actually improving. That is not what happened here. Cost per conversation dropped 23% in the interest-based campaign and 44% in the broad one, independently. Both engines got cheaper, and the account average followed.

Four of the nine test creatives shipped under Meta’s default ad name, which is how the history became unreadable in the first place. Every ad now ships as concept_variant_date, so the next audit reads itself.

What’s next

The next gain is in the audience, not the creative.

  • Rebuild the broad audience instead of testing more creative against it. At four times the cost of the interest-based campaign, that is where the remaining money is.
  • Hold one test slot a week on the interest-based campaign, so the winning bench keeps being challenged.
  • Instrument the handoff. Conversation volume is measurable; what happens after it is not. A weekly count of quoted jobs from the client would turn this from a cost metric into a revenue one.

Method

How I measured it, and what it does not prove.

  • Two equal 15-day windows, 9–23 September against 24 September–8 October 2026, exported from Ads Manager at campaign, ad set and ad level.
  • Meta-reported conversations started, 7-day click / 1-day view attribution. The client shares no sales data, so no CPA or ROAS claim is made here.
  • Ad-level decisions rest on 2 to 13 conversations per creative. That is directional, not conclusive, which is why testing is still running rather than finished.
  • A before/after read on a live account is not a controlled experiment: seasonality and auction conditions are not isolated.
  • Cost per conversation is still above the sister accounts (ARS 1,500–3,000). The interest-based campaign is now inside that band; the broad audience is not.
  • Pesos converted at ARS 1,555 per USD, 8 October 2026. This is a small-budget account — about USD 19 a day per ad set — so the result is about efficiency, not scale.
