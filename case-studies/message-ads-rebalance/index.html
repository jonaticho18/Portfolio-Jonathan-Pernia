More conversations on a smaller budget

An Instagram Direct lead account was handing three quarters of one ad set’s budget to its most expensive creative. I paused it, rebuilt the creative bench and concentrated spend where conversations were actually cheap. Over the next 15 days the account produced 16% more conversations on 28% less spend.

The account

The client sells and installs high-ticket security doors and gates. Every lead arrives as an Instagram Direct conversation, so the account optimises for conversations started, not for purchases — the client never reports back which conversations turned into sales. Two always-on campaigns, both ABO: one aimed at a broad mid-value audience, one at interest-based audiences around gates. I have administered this account and two sister accounts for three years.

This account had been running the same way since March. Nobody had asked for a review, which is usually when an account is worth reviewing.

What the audit found

  • One creative was absorbing 72.6% of the ad set’s budget (ARS 523,736 of 721,064) while delivering conversations at 12,470 — 2.5× the account average.
  • Two creatives were spending with zero conversations, and a third had one conversation for 9,939.
  • The creative history was unreadable. New assets had been swapped inside existing ads, so a single ad name covered several different videos and no before/after could be reconstructed from the account itself.
  • Budget was allocated by inertia, not by cost per conversation: the expensive audience held the larger daily budget.

The real finding

The account was not underperforming because of the creatives it had. It was underperforming because the worst creative had the most money, and nothing in the setup was going to correct that on its own.

What I changed

  1. 23 Sep
    Paused the budget holder

    Reel10 off. Its spend was released to the creatives already converting below the ad set average.

  2. 24 Sep
    Rebuilt the creative bench

    Nine creatives into test on the mid-value audience — four door-focused reels, a multi-product cut and four engagement variants — each launched as a new ad instead of a swap inside an old one.

  3. 25 Sep
    Shifted weight to the cheap audience

    The interest-based gate campaign kept its budget while the broad audience lost 40% of its spend. Conversations, not impressions, decided the split.

  4. 1 Oct
    Cut the losers, kept three

    Every variant above the ad set average was paused once it had delivered enough conversations to read. Three stayed live.

  5. 8 Oct
    Read the result at equal windows

    15 days before the change against 15 days after, same account, same objective, same attribution setting.

What the numbers say

  • The efficiency engine is the interest-based campaign. At ARS 1,935 (USD 1.24) per conversation it is roughly four times cheaper than the broad audience, and it absorbed the extra volume without its cost rising.
  • The broad mid-value audience is structurally expensive, even after a 42% improvement. The next move there is audience work, not more creative testing.
  • Creative concentration was the whole problem. No new budget, no new audience and no new objective were needed to take 38% out of the cost per conversation.
  • Process was part of the fix. Four of the nine test creatives shipped under Meta’s default ad name, which is how the history became unreadable in the first place. Every ad now ships as concept_variant_date, so the next audit reads itself.

Method and limits

  • Two equal 15-day windows, 9–23 September against 24 September–8 October 2026. The final day is partial, so the second window is reported slightly short.
  • Meta-reported conversations started, 7-day click / 1-day view attribution, exported from Ads Manager at campaign, ad set and ad level.
  • The client does not share sales data, so cost per conversation is the working KPI. No CPA or ROAS claim is made here.
  • Ad-level decisions rest on 2 to 13 conversations per creative. That is directional, not conclusive, which is why testing is still running rather than finished.
  • This is a before/after read on a live account, not a controlled experiment: seasonality and auction conditions are not isolated.
  • Cost per conversation is still above where the sister accounts sit (ARS 1,500–3,000, or USD 0.96–1.93). The interest-based campaign is now inside that band; the broad audience is not.
  • Peso figures are converted at ARS 1,555 per USD, the selling rate on 8 October 2026. This is a small-budget account — ARS 30,000 a day per ad set, about USD 19 — so the result is about efficiency, not scale.

Next 15 days

  • Rebuild the mid-value audience instead of testing more creative against it.
  • Hold one test slot per week on the interest-based campaign, so the winning bench keeps being challenged.
  • Instrument the handoff: conversation volume is measurable, what happens after it is not. Getting even a weekly count of quoted jobs back from the client would turn this from a cost metric into a revenue one.
